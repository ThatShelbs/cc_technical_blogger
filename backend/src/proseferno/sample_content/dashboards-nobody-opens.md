# Your Dashboard Has a Funeral Scheduled

*Most dashboards die quietly. Here is how to tell if yours is already gone.*

Last spring I audited our analytics portal. Sixty-one dashboards. I pulled the access logs expecting a few stragglers. Instead, forty-four of them had not been opened in ninety days. One had been opened exactly once, by me, during the audit.

That is a 72 percent mortality rate, and nobody sent flowers.

## The Dashboard Life Cycle

Every dashboard follows the same arc. Someone senior asks a question in a meeting. An analyst, who is lovely and overworked, builds a dashboard to answer it. The answer is "interesting." The question changes. The dashboard stays.

Nobody deletes it, because deleting feels risky. What if somebody needs it? So it sits there, refreshing nightly, quietly burning warehouse credits to serve an audience of zero.

I call these zombie dashboards. They are not dead. They are worse than dead. They are *scheduled*.

## Three Signs Yours Is a Zombie

1. **Nobody can say who it is for.** If the owner field says "Analytics Team," it is for no one.
2. **It answers a question from two reorganizations ago.** Check the title. If it mentions a product that has since been renamed, you know.
3. **It has more filters than users.** Twelve dropdowns is not flexibility. It is a confession.

## What To Do Instead

Start with the decision, not the data. Before you build anything, ask: "What will you do differently if this number goes up?" If the answer is a shrug, you are about to build a zombie.

Then give every dashboard a name, an owner and an expiry date. I put a banner at the top that says *Review by March 1*. When the date arrives, the owner either renews it or lets it go. Renewal takes one click. Letting go takes none.

The first quarter we tried this, we retired thirty-one dashboards. Not one person complained. One person thanked us, which I am choosing to count as a complaint-shaped compliment.

## The Takeaway

A dashboard is not an asset just because it exists. It is a promise that someone will look at it. Make fewer promises, and keep them.

If you take one thing from this post, take this: go open your analytics portal, sort by last viewed, and look at the bottom of the list. It is going to be a quiet afternoon.
